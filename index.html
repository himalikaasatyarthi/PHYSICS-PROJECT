WHY IT MATTERS

Coordinate systems in physics

Choose a geometry that matches the symmetry of the problem.

◎

Spherical symmetry

Useful for fields around a point charge or a spherical mass distribution.

E = kQ / r²
Example: electric field of a point charge
◉

Cylindrical symmetry

Useful for fields around a long straight current-carrying wire.

B = μ₀I / 2πr
Example: magnetic field around a wire
↗

Polar motion

Useful for describing motion along a circular path using radius and angle.

v = rω
Example: uniform circular motion
QUICK REFERENCE

Understand the coordinates

Polar

Two-dimensional position using distance r from the origin and angle θ from the positive X-axis.

x = r cos θy = r sin θ
Cylindrical

Three-dimensional position using radius r, azimuth θ and height z.

x = r cos θy = r sin θz = z
Spherical

Three-dimensional position using distance ρ, azimuth θ and polar angle φ from +Z.

x = ρ sin φ cos θy = ρ sin φ sin θz = ρ cos φ
